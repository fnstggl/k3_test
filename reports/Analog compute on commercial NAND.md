# Test modes lock NAND's analog adder away

No commercially manufactured NAND die has been shown, through publicly documented means, to compute y = Σ wᵢxᵢ by summing independently controlled string currents on a shared bitline at the same time and recovering a 6–10-bit number through its own sense path. On the public record the answer is **not established**. For an outside experimenter limited to public commands it is **no**. The physics is already inside stock silicon. On **160 commercial 48-layer TLC dies, Flash-Cosmos activated 1–32 blocks on one bitline in a single sensing**, but it used proprietary vendor test-mode commands, and the output was a 1-bit wired-OR ([Flash-Cosmos](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.05566)). **Kioxia's own engineers computed 1-bit and 3-bit × 128-dimension inner products on 3D flash using the on-chip sense amplifier.** They activated blocks *sequentially*, not simultaneously, through non-public control, and read results as threshold decisions at swept sense times rather than as a numerical conversion ([IEEE 11026979](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11026979); [IEEE 11532787](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11532787)). Seoul National University (SNU) and SK hynix measured real bitline currents on "unmodified commercial" V-NAND, but only by probing SK hynix-supplied wafers with a parameter analyzer, and they simulated the multi-string sum and the ADC ([PMC13137843](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13137843/)). What public documentation lacks can be stated exactly: no public command puts strings from two blocks or sub-blocks of the same plane on one bitline at once, no public command touches sense time, precharge or the sense-amp reference, and the only public lever on the read reference is a handful of preset, uncalibrated read-retry modes on certain Micron, Macronix and SK hynix MLC parts ([Linux nand_micron.c](https://elixir.bootlin.com/linux/v6.17/source/drivers/mtd/nand/raw/nand_micron.c#L39)). The cheapest realistic path has two tiers: a socket-plus-FPGA rig costing about $700–1,500 that does everything public commands allow, then a partnership with a group that already holds test-mode access to run the simultaneous 2→8→32-string ladder. Uncertainty remains because the ONFI and JEDEC specifications and NDA-only datasheets could not be read, and nobody has published summed-current linearity measured through a commercial die's own sense path.

## A. Verdict: stock dies already sum string currents, but no public command reaches the sum

The verdict rests on a chain of evidence that breaks at capability (2). In planar and 3D NAND, **thousands of strings in a plane share each bitline**, and Flash-Cosmos states the consequence directly: sensing wordlines in different blocks at once yields a bitwise OR, because any conducting string discharges the shared sense-out node ([Flash-Cosmos §1, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.05566)). SanDisk's patents describe the stock all-bit-line sense amplifier as a clamped bitline feeding a small sense capacitor. The bitline current discharges that capacitor, and the "demarcation current … can be set by appropriate adjustment of the discharging period" ([US 7,046,568](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7046568B2/en)). The sense node therefore integrates *total* bitline current: when several strings conduct together, the node physically sees their sum (inference from the circuit description). The adder exists. The obstacles are three: the decoders select one block (and one sub-block) per plane in normal operation, the readout is one comparator bit per strobe, and every knob that would turn that comparator into a converter is an internal trim.

Taken capability by capability, the public record is thin. **Wordline bias (1)** is reachable only as preset read-retry modes (Micron feature 89h, Macronix feature 89h with six modes, and SK hynix 1x-nm MLC register writes), all cell-side and all with undisclosed voltages ([Linux v6.17 drivers](https://elixir.bootlin.com/linux/v6.17/source/drivers/mtd/nand/raw/nand_macronix.c#L13)); MCFlash used user-mode read offsets on 50 commercial 3D chips but does not disclose the feature addresses ([MCFlash §5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05119)). **Independent string or block selection on a shared bitline (2)** is absent from every public command set: multi-plane reads use different planes and therefore different bitlines, and Kioxia's public Multi Page Read allows "maximum one block … from each District" ([TC58NVG2S0HTA00 pp. 33–35](https://storage.googleapis.com/graviton-electric-symbols/document_assets/lcsc/1912111437_KIOXIA-TC58NVG2S0HTA00_C438944.pdf)). **Simultaneous summation (3)** appears on commercial dies only as Flash-Cosmos's test-mode wired-OR. **Access to the analog sum (4)** has never been shown through a die's own pins; the SNU work reached analog current only with external instruments on probe pads ([PMC13502362 §4.2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13502362/)). **Numerical conversion (5)** has been shown for single cells by repeated reads at shifted references ([Cai et al. DATE 2013](https://people.inf.ethz.ch/omutlu/pub/flash-memory-voltage-characterization_date13.pdf)), and for sums only inside Kioxia, as coarse threshold classes ("inner product ≤ 49 with >99% probability") ([Kioxia topic-93](https://www.kioxia.com/en-jp/rd/technology/topics/topics-93.html)).

Seven distinctions decide how each piece of evidence counts. The table applies them to the record.

| Distinction | What the evidence shows | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Several wordlines in one series string (AND-like) vs independently controlled strings in parallel on one sense node (Kirchhoff sum) | Flash-Cosmos intra-block sensing of up to 48 WLs is an AND. SNU TLCAM drives 32 WLs in 4 reads. SanDisk's FIG. 15A "calculation cells" bias several WLs of one string, giving a series-resistance function. | Sequential accumulation vs simultaneous summation | Kioxia's block pulses are "one after another, without overlap", so charge integrates on the bitline over time ([topic-85 Fig. 1](https://www.kioxia.com/content/dam/kioxia/ja-jp/rd/technology/topics/img/topics-85_img_001.png)). The case for "yes" deserves a hearing. Flash-Cosmos argues that "manufacturers commonly design a chip to support the activation of multiple WLs … and multiple blocks … critical for rapid testing of the chip". Its own chips did support this in test mode ([Flash-Cosmos §4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.05566)). Kioxia calls its inner-product scheme "fully compatible with conventional 3D flash memory" ([topic-85](https://www.kioxia.com/en-jp/rd/technology/topics/topics-85.html)). 3D-aCortex claims VMM on "existing 3D-NAND flash memory blocks, without any need for their modification" ([arXiv 1908.02472](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02472)). Each claim concerns hardware, not access. Flash-Cosmos supports its "commonly" with a 2010 textbook, and MCFlash counters that inter-block activation is "an uncommon practice in current NAND flash chip designs" and that "existing NAND chips typically support only single-wordline sensing" ([MCFlash §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05119)). Kioxia's own wording is "no additional WL control circuit is required", which means no new drivers, not a public command ([IEEE 11026979](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11026979)). 3D-aCortex leaves the array unmodified but adds new time-domain periphery, and it is simulation-only.

**Confidence.** High that no public standard, datasheet or mainline driver documents same-plane multi-block activation or sense-node control. This is a negative search across Linux v6.17, U-Boot v2025.10, an ONFI 3.1-derived header and every public datasheet obtained. Lower confidence that ONFI 4.x/5.x and JESD230 contain nothing relevant, because onfi.org and jedec.org blocked every download ([JEDEC JESD230 listing](https://www.jedec.org/standards-documents/results/jesd230); [Wikipedia ONFI history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_NAND_Flash_Interface_Working_Group)). Unknown what current 3D datasheets distributed under NDA contain. Low knowledge of how many bits a stock sense path could resolve from a sum: no linearity, noise or temperature data exist for summed current through a commercial die's own sense amplifier.

## E. A staged experiment that stays under $2,000 until the vendor step

The goal is to test whether 2, then 8, then 32 independently controlled string contributions can be summed and recovered numerically. Public commands can do every part of that except the sum itself. The plan therefore builds a public-command rig that measures everything the analog experiment will need: weight-cell thresholds, read-level resolution, binary contributions and a digitally emulated reference dot product. It then hands the simultaneous step to a partner who holds test-mode access. **Pin-level control never reaches internal sense timing**: changing operating parameters such as "the ISPP step voltage and other timing parameters" required test-mode commands even for Flash-Cosmos ([Flash-Cosmos §5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.05566)), and from the pins tR is visible only as R/B# busy time (inference from the public interface).

### What the vendor stage must deliver, and what to expect from it

The vendor-stage protocol is a k-of-N ladder (proposed here; nobody has published it). For each N in {2, 8, 32}, program the N blocks or sub-blocks sharing a bitline so that exactly k strings conduct, for k = 0…N. Activate all N at once. Sweep the integration time, or the reference, and record each bitline's flip time. Kioxia's sequential data give the expected shape: flip time falling roughly as 1/inner-product, and "BL voltage drop … increases linearly with the number of activated blocks" ([IEEE 11532787](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11532787)). The cumulative-probability curves span roughly ×1.5–2 per level, which suggests coarse classes rather than 6–10 clean bits without calibration (inference from the published figures). Time-to-threshold coding is 1/I-nonlinear, so at µA-scale full-scale currents the top codes would be separated by very small differences in trip time. Expect the useful resolution to come from fixing the per-string current (a CC cell), multi-level weights and repeated strobes, not from a single sense.

Four physical limits need a plan. **Power and latency** rise with activated blocks: inter-block power stays below that of an erase only up to about four blocks, latency is 36.3% above tR at 32 blocks, and Flash-Cosmos recommends at most four simultaneous blocks for fixed latency ([Flash-Cosmos](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.05566)). **String-current spread** comes from top-select-gate polysilicon grain boundaries ([APS 2026](https://wulixb.iphy.ac.cn/article/doi/10.7498/aps.75.20260199)). **Temperature** strongly changes bitline current ([PMC12484496](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12484496/)). **Source-line bias** is serious enough that SanDisk's multi-pass sensing exists to suppress it ([US 7,046,568](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7046568B2/en)).

Two partners could run stages V1–V2 with little new work (inference). The Flash-Cosmos authors already drive 1–32-block simultaneous sensing and change "other timing parameters" on the same chip population. Kioxia's IMW team already sweeps T_SEN on product-class silicon with sequential activation. V4 requires vendor wafers and a probe station; the notes record no prices for that equipment. Results obtained under an NDA may not be publishable, so publication rights belong in any agreement.

## Conclusion

The barrier between commercial NAND and analog matrix-vector multiplication is administrative and architectural, not physical. Every planar and 3D die already wires thousands of strings to a current-integrating sense node, and two independent teams have exercised that node with multiple strings or blocks: one in test mode, one in-house. What the industry has never exposed is the **control plane**: simultaneous same-plane selection and the sense-node timing that sets the trip current. Simultaneous multi-string selection recurs in Kioxia, Micron, SanDisk and Samsung patents, and sense time already exists as a die or controller trim in Micron and SanDisk patents. The most useful question for a vendor is therefore narrow: "may we sweep integration time during a multi-block read on dies you already ship?" It does not require new silicon, which makes it a more answerable request than a request for a CIM chip.

The numerical target reshapes the experiment more than the hardware does. Binary weights and inputs cap N strings at log₂(N + 1) bits, so 32 strings cannot exceed about 5 bits even with perfect analog sensing. Six to ten bits require multi-level cells, time-coded inputs (Kioxia's approach) or hundreds to thousands of contributions. Each of these collides with measured limits: the power cost of simultaneous activation, the string-current spread from select-gate polysilicon, temperature sensitivity, and the 1/I compression of time-to-threshold coding. The realistic near-term result is therefore not a 6–10-bit analog dot product on a retail part. It is a public-command rig that produces calibrated per-cell weights and exact digital reference sums, paired with a partner-run k-of-N ladder that measures how many of those sums a stock sense amplifier can actually separate.
