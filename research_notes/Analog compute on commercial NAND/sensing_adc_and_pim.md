# NAND sensing circuitry as an ADC for summed bitline currents, and what recent NAND-PIM proposals add vs. what production dies provide

Scope note: these notes use only public material (papers, arXiv, patents, public kernel/driver code, press/tip sheets). No vendor NDA documents were sought or used. Labels used below: **[MEASURED-COTS]** = measured on commercial off-the-shelf dies; **[MEASURED-CUSTOM]** = measured on custom/test silicon; **[SIM]** = simulation/model only; **[PATENT]** = patent disclosure (not evidence of a shipping feature); **[PUBLIC-CMD]** = reachable through publicly documented commands.

## Q1. How do modern NAND sense amplifiers work, and what are typical values?

### Takeaway
Production NAND page buffers are per-bitline (or per-bitline-pair) **binary comparators with a latch**: the bitline and a small sense/"SO/SEN" node are precharged, the selected string is allowed to conduct (or not) at a fixed wordline read voltage, and a strobe compares the sense-node voltage with a fixed trip/reference point after a set time; the result is one bit per sensing phase. In the all-bitline (ABL) current-sensing style, the bitline is clamped (~0.5 V) and a ~30 fF sense capacitor is discharged by the cell current, so the **effective demarcation current (~100–1000 nA) is set by the discharge (integration) time** — i.e., there is an intrinsic current-to-time relationship, but it is used as a 1-bit threshold, not as a numerical measurement.

### Cited Findings
**ABL vs. even/odd (shielded) architectures**
- SanDisk/Toshiba presented all-bitline (ABL) access at ISSCC 2008; ABL's programming-throughput advantage is credited to "current sensing" as opposed to the "conventional" voltage-sensing scheme. Conventional NAND used an even/odd bitline architecture (two BLs per sense amp); ABL uses one BL per sense amp; max MLC program throughput 10 MB/s (even/odd) vs 34 MB/s (ABL); 2 vs 8 pages of 4 KB programmed in parallel — [IEEE SCV SSCS chapter, Apr 2008 talk by R.-A. Cernea (SanDisk)](https://r6.ieee.org/scv-sscs/?p=314); [slide deck](https://ewh.ieee.org/r6/scv/ssc/pdfs/Apr08.pdf)
- Primary paper: R.-A. Cernea et al., "A 34 MB/s MLC Write Throughput 16 Gb NAND With All Bit Line Architecture on 56 nm Technology," IEEE JSSC 44(1):186–194, 2009; ISSCC 2008 version "34MB/s-Program-Throughput 16Gb MLC NAND with All-Bitline Architecture in 56nm," pp. 420–421 (SanDisk/Toshiba, 56 nm 2D MLC) — [dblp](https://dblp.org/pid/135/2172). (Full text not retrieved.)
- ABL requires keeping a constant voltage on all bitlines during evaluation and sensing, with a DC path through conducting cells; an NTHU thesis notes 3D cell currents are much smaller than planar ones, motivating ABL for 3D, but ABL costs area (their simplified ABL: −23.1% layout area for ~2.27% read-throughput penalty) — [NTHU thesis](https://scholars.nthu.edu.tw/esploro/outputs/graduate/Low-Cost-and-Disturbance-Free-for/9957797015406774)
- Patents describe the ABL bitline potential being held at a fixed voltage so adjacent-BL effects are excluded and all BLs can be sensed in parallel; current-detection sense amps allow high-speed sensing but draw more power because of continuous read current — [US8203888 (via USPTO)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8203888); [US8169830B2 "Sensing for all bit line architecture"](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8169830B2/en) (search-result snippets only; claim text not reviewed)

**ABL sense-amp circuit values (SanDisk patent, 2D era) [PATENT]**
- US7046568B2 (Cernea, SanDisk; priority 2002-09-24), "Memory sensing circuit and method for low voltage operation": bitline clamp holds V_BL at "e.g., 0.5 v"; dedicated sense capacitor "e.g., 30 fF" versus "a bit line capacitance of about 2 pF"; cell conduction current discharges the capacitor during a strobe-defined period; demarcation currents "typically in the range 100–1000 nA" (example passes at 300 nA and 100 nA; precharge current limit 500 nA); the demarcation current "can be set by appropriate adjustment of the discharging period"; preferred embodiment senses in three passes, the first two identifying and shutting off high-current cells to reduce **source-line bias** for the final pass; designed for all-bit-line sensing — [Google Patents US7046568B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7046568B2/en)
- US7733704B2 ("power-saving multi-pass sensing"): cell current discharges a capacitor so the SEN node falls from VDD at a rate set by the current; the result is latched by a strobe — [Google Patents US7733704B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7733704B2/en) (search snippet)
- US11907545B2 (SanDisk; priority 2021-09-16): sense node charged to Vsense_init "such as 3 V"; BL biased to Vsense ≈ 0.8 V via a selector gate at Vsense+Vth (~1.5 V); "an amount of decay of the sense node is used to determine whether a memory cell is in a conductive or non-conductive state"; comparison against a trip voltage at a sense time — [Google Patents US11907545B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11907545B2/en)

**Modern 3D NAND read phases and measured timing [MEASURED-COTS, vendor anonymized]**
- Park et al. (ASPLOS 2021) describe a 3D NAND read as precharge → evaluation → discharge: in precharge "a NAND flash chip charges each target BL and its sense-out (SO) capacitor C_SO to a specific voltage V_PRE" while V_REF is applied to the selected WL; in evaluation the chip "disconnects the BL from V_PRE" and enables the SA; for an erased cell "charge in C_SO quickly flows through the BL, which rapidly decreases the SO-node voltage below the SA's reference voltage V_SR"; unselected WLs get V_PASS (>6 V) — [Park et al., arXiv 2104.09611](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09611)
- tR = N_SENSE × (tPRE + tEVAL + tDISCH); N_SENSE = 1 for SLC and up to 3 for a TLC page; on 160 **48-layer 3D TLC** chips the defaults are ⟨tPRE, tEVAL, tDISCH⟩ = ⟨24 µs, 5 µs, 10 µs⟩ (≈5:1:2), i.e., ~39 µs per sensing; tEVAL is only ~1/8 of tR — [Park et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09611)
- Flash-Cosmos (MICRO 2022) on 160 48-layer 3D TLC chips: precharge charges target BLs and SO capacitors to V_PRE, evaluation disconnects V_PRE and enables the latch; tR = 22.5 µs and tPROG = 200 µs (SLC-mode); separate sensing latch and cache latch — [Flash-Cosmos, arXiv 2209.05566](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.05566)
- MCFlash (2026) measured page-read latency of ~40–90 µs across tested COTS 3D NAND (64-layer FG and 176-layer CT, "a major vendor"); supply-current traces for MSB pages show "an initial pre-charge spike followed by two distinct sensing intervals"; sensing energy "scales linearly with the number of sensing phases" — [MCFlash, arXiv 2605.05119](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05119)
- Low-latency SLC products: Samsung Z-NAND tR ≈ 3 µs (Cheong et al., ISSCC 2018, cited and assumed by LLM-on-the-Palm) — [LLM-on-the-Palm, ICCAD 2025, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCAD66269.2025.11240826) (local PDF in repo); a reference listing describes Z-NAND as 15–20× faster than conventional NAND — [IEEE Xplore 8310322 refs](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8310322/references) (search snippet)
- PIM papers' assumptions: conventional 3D NAND read latency 20–50 µs (2511.12860) — [arXiv 2511.12860](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12860); tR 30 µs in Cambricon-LLM's table (20 µs in a worked example) — [Cambricon-LLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15654v1); KVNAND assumes SLC tR = 4 µs — [KVNAND](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03608v1); NVLLM assumes 5.12 µs per plane — [NVLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25699)
- A Kioxia-style patent family gives illustrative TLC lower-page read times of ~36 µs (fast read), ~40 µs (normal) and ~120 µs (DLA read) — [USPTO 11099783](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11099783) / [USPTO 12159061](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12159061) (seen only via search summary; exact document and assignee not verified)
- Recent ISSCC context (no tR found): Samsung ISSCC 2025 paper 30.1 "A 28Gb/mm² 4xx-layer 1Tb 3b/cell WF-bonding 3D-NAND flash with 5.6Gb/s/pin IOs" — [ComputerBase preview](https://www.computerbase.de/news/storage/isscc-2025-samsung-bereitet-4xx-layer-v10-tlc-nand-mit-5-6-gb-s-vor.90485/); Kioxia/SanDisk ISSCC 2025: 4.8 Gb/s NAND interface — [SanDisk press release](https://www.sandisk.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/2025/kioxia-and-sandisk-unveil-next-generation-3d-flash-memory-technology); Samsung ISSCC 2024 280-layer 1Tb QLC with 3.2 Gb/s IO (cited by LLM-on-the-Palm refs [9],[16]) — [LLM-on-the-Palm](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCAD66269.2025.11240826)

**Cell/string current and bitline physics**
- 3D string current degrades with stack height: polysilicon channel series resistance increases, "causing total read current to saturate," compressing state separation and sense margin; sub-block partitioning / dual-deck mitigate at the cost of extra select transistors; V_pass ≈ 6–8 V; self-heating gives "temperature gradients of several kelvins across the stack height" — [Venkatesan et al. (Georgia Tech/Samsung/AMAT et al.), "The future of 3D NAND flash technology," arXiv 2610.06363, Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06363v1)
- Conventional NAND bitlines are "generally several millimeters long" (vs 144 µm in a CIM-optimized design) — [Shim et al., MEMSYS 2020](https://www.memsys.io/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/p80-shim.pdf)

### Inferences
- The production sense amp is fundamentally a **time-gated 1-bit current comparator**: I_trip ≈ C_SEN·ΔV_trip / t_int. With the SanDisk example values (C_SEN ≈ 30 fF, demarcation 100–1000 nA) and an assumed trip swing of ~0.5–1 V (my assumption; no public number found), t_int would be tens to a few hundred ns — consistent with tEVAL (~5 µs on a 48L TLC part) being a small fraction of tR, which is dominated by bitline precharge (24 µs) and discharge (10 µs) of ~pF-class, mm-long bitlines.
- Because the bitline in ABL is clamped, the SEN capacitor integrates the *total* bitline current — so physically, if several strings on the same bitline conduct simultaneously, the SEN node does see their sum. What production dies lack (publicly) is (a) a way to turn on several independently-selected strings on one bitline at once in normal mode, and (b) any readout other than one comparator decision per strobe.
- Even/odd shielded (voltage) sensing (older 2D) lets the bitline itself discharge and then senses its voltage; ABL current sensing holds the bitline steady. Park et al.'s 3D description (SO-node discharging below V_SR) is generic and does not reveal which flavor that vendor uses.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve full texts of Cernea JSSC 2009 or Micheloni/Crippa/Marelli "Inside NAND Flash Memories" (Springer 2010; cited by Park et al. as ref [68] for the precharge/evaluate/discharge model) or Compagnoni et al. Proc. IEEE 2017 — so no textbook-sourced SEN capacitance, bitline precharge voltage or sense-time numbers for 3D generations.
- No public per-vendor 3D NAND sense-node capacitance, bitline capacitance, V_PRE, or strobe time found for Samsung, Kioxia/WD, Micron, SK hynix or YMTC ISSCC papers (searches for ISSCC 2023–2025 tR values also failed to return tR figures).
- Typical 3D cell current at read bias for commercial parts (tens–hundreds of nA) not found in a primary vendor source; the 100–1000 nA demarcation range is from a 2002-priority 2D-era patent.

## Q2. Which sensing parameters are trimmable, and which are user-exposed in public documentation vs. patents only?

### Takeaway
Publicly exposed knobs are **cell-side**: read-reference (wordline) voltage offsets / read-retry modes and soft-bit read, reached through ONFI SET/GET FEATURES with vendor-specific feature addresses. **Sense-node-side** parameters (integration/sense time, precharge time, trip reference) exist as die trims (patents describe them stored in ROM fuses/registers and calibrated) and research groups with FPGA testers have altered read-timing parameters or used test-mode commands — but I found no public datasheet that documents user control of sense time, SEN precharge level or sense-amp reference current.

### Cited Findings
- **Public read-retry (cell-side)**: Micron "provides READ RETRY support via the ONFI vendor-specific parameter block … to indicate how many read-retry modes are available" using "the ONFI {GET,SET}_FEATURES commands with a vendor-specific feature address" = 89h; "Tested on Micron MT29F32G08CBADA, which supports 8 read-retry modes"; mode 0 restores default — [Linux MTD patch, B. Norris, Jan 2014](https://lists.infradead.org/pipermail/linux-mtd/2014-January/051525.html) [PUBLIC-CMD]
- **Public read-offset + soft-bit read on 3D COTS**: MCFlash states COTS raw NAND chips "provide user-accessible commands to shift this reference voltage" (read offset V_OFF, positive or negative) and Soft Bit Read (two extra references, internal XNOR to form a soft bit); "Shifted Read (or Read Retry) and SBR are standard features in NAND flash, typically supported through user-accessible commands"; their board used ONFI-standard commands including "read reference shifting, soft bit reading, and read retry via SET FEATURE user-mode commands"; changing offsets costs "less than 10 µs" — [MCFlash, Colorado State, arXiv 2605.05119, May 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05119) [MEASURED-COTS, PUBLIC-CMD]
- **Read-timing parameters via SET FEATURE on a research tester**: Park et al.'s FPGA controller "supports … dynamic change of timing parameters for a read by using the SET FEATURE command" (citing the public ONFI 4.2 spec); they reduced tPRE/tEVAL/tDISCH and found tPRE can be cut ≥40% (→25% lower tR) safely, but "it is very cost-ineffective to reduce tEVAL. Reducing tEVAL by 20% introduces 30 additional bit errors" per KiB (41.7% of ECC capability) even on fresh pages; "manufacturers set the default tPRE more pessimistically" — [Park et al., ASPLOS 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09611) [MEASURED-COTS; feature address/vendor not disclosed in the text I extracted]
- **Test-mode (proprietary) commands**: Flash-Cosmos' FPGA controller supports "various test-mode commands necessary to dynamically change operating parameters" and to "simultaneously activate multiple WLs"; vendors "consider their test-mode command set design to be proprietary" — [Flash-Cosmos](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.05566) [MEASURED-COTS via non-public commands]
- MCFlash characterizes ParaBit as needing changed timing-control logic ("a privileged command sequence"), and Flash-Cosmos MWS/ESP as needing vendor support or design changes, i.e., not usable on COTS chips with user-mode commands — [MCFlash](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05119)
- **Integration time as a die parameter [PATENT]**: WD/SanDisk US11430531B2 (priority 2020-09-08): "An integration time is the length of time for which the charge on a sense node is allowed to change"; "changing the integration time may be the equivalent of a slight change to the read reference voltage"; default integration times kept in registers/ROM fuses/die storage, "copied … when the memory die 300 is powered on," and "calibrated in response to a decoding error" (or after P/E counts) via BER-estimation scans/valley search — [Google Patents US11430531B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11430531B2/en)
- **Demarcation current set by discharge period [PATENT]**: US7046568B2 — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7046568B2/en)
- **Default read references are factory-calibrated**: MCFlash notes the default read reference "is already highly optimized by the manufacturer," accounts for layer-to-layer differences and in some devices is "dynamically adjusted based on P/E cycling" — [MCFlash](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05119)

### Inferences
- The only sensing control a non-vendor can rely on from public documentation is the **wordline read voltage** (offset/retry tables, sometimes a few discrete modes — 8 on the Micron part above). This is a cell-side change: it moves the Vth threshold of the selected cell, not the current threshold of the sense node. For one conducting string the two are equivalent-ish (US11430531 says a small integration-time change ≈ a small read-reference change), but for a *sum* of string currents they are not interchangeable: a WL offset changes every cell's current nonlinearly, whereas a sense-time/trip change scales the comparison current.
- Sense-time/precharge trims clearly exist on production dies (Park et al. could change them; patents describe fused defaults), but evidence of access is via research FPGA platforms of groups with industry ties and unspecified feature addresses — not a vendor-published, user-facing feature.

### Gaps
- No public datasheet found listing feature addresses for sense time, SEN precharge, BL precharge level, or sense-amp reference current (searched Micron/ONFI read-retry docs; found only read-retry 89h). Current Micron/Kioxia/Samsung/SK hynix/YMTC 3D-NAND datasheets are generally distributed under NDA, so their contents are unknown here.
- Read-offset DAC resolution (mV per step) and range for any 3D part not found publicly.

## Q3. Can multiple strobes / varied sense time within one array access produce multi-bit values? Does each extra bit need another full read?

### Takeaway
**In shipped operation and public commands, each additional read level is another sensing phase** (precharge → evaluate → discharge, ~tens of µs on standard 3D TLC), so extra precision costs roughly linear tR. **Patents** (Anobit/Apple, SanDisk/WD) describe multi-strobe sensing in one bitline discharge — two strobes for hard+soft bits, or several integration times (t±Δt, t±2Δt) under one wordline voltage — which is exactly a time-domain multi-level comparison of sense-node current. None of these patents claim it is user-accessible, and none target summed currents from multiple strings.

### Cited Findings
- tR = N_SENSE × (tPRE + tEVAL + tDISCH); "Since each retry step of a read-retry operation is effectively the same as a regular page read," retries can only be pipelined with CACHE READ, not merged — [Park et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09611) [MEASURED-COTS]
- MCFlash: SBR "performs soft sensing by reading the same page twice: once with V_REF− and once with V_REF+," combined by an internal XNOR; XNOR needs "up to four sensing phases"; XNOR consumes ~51% more energy per kB than AND (one sensing step) — [MCFlash](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05119) [MEASURED-COTS]
- Dong, Xie, Zhang (RPI/Skyera/Intel), IEEE TCAS-I 60(9), 2013: "NAND flash memory on-chip sensing latency is linearly proportional to the required sensing quantization granularity," and flash-to-controller transfer latency grows accordingly — [Dong et al. 2013](https://sites.ecse.rpi.edu/~tzhang/pub/LatencyTCASI2013.pdf) [model]
- **Multiple sense times without re-precharging [PATENT]**: Anobit/Apple US8493783B2 (priority 2008-03-18): a bit line "is sensed with multiple sense times without re-charging the bit line between the read operations"; device "senses the bit line current multiple times during the same discharge cycle"; longer integration averages read noise; example "long sense time may be on the order of 25 µS and the short sense time … 10 µS"; each sense produces hard binary results (multi-level information still comes from read thresholds) — [Google Patents US8493783B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8493783B2/en)
- **Hard + soft bit from one discharge [PATENT]**: SanDisk US11907545B2 describes combining hard and soft sensing into one precharge/discharge event with the sense node strobed twice — a first strobe after a discharge interval and a second after an additional interval to separate the hard-bit/soft-bit ranges (per search summary: "a second strobe after a longer interval separates the HB-to-SB range") — [Google Patents US11907545B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11907545B2/en)
- **Several integration times under one WL bias [PATENT]**: WD US11430531B2: "sensing the set of memory cells for different integration times while applying the one or more reference voltages," example t−2Δt, t−Δt, t, t+Δt, t+2Δt — [Google Patents US11430531B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11430531B2/en)
- **Vendor patent that would need a new multi-level SA for summed strings [PATENT]**: SanDisk US11328204B2 (priority 2018-07-24; inventors W. H. Choi, P.-F. Chiu, W. Ma, M. Qin, G. J. Hemink, M. Lueker-Boden): in standard sensing "a sense amplifier will either register as conducting ('1') or not conducting ('0')"; summation is done by a digital counter; a multi-block embodiment "lets multiple unit synapses along a common bit line from differing block to be sensed in parallel" with "the introduction of multi-bit sense amplifiers" ("such as one that can distinguish between different current level"); parallelism limited by "the amount of the resultant current that would be drawn and the level of resolution that can reasonably achieved by the multi-bit sense amplifiers from the available current window"; mechanism of the multi-bit SA not specified — [Google Patents US11328204B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11328204B2/en)

### Inferences
- Within one access, the sense node gives one comparator decision per strobe. A k-bit numerical value of a summed current would need ≥k (SAR-like, requiring a programmable reference) or up to 2^k−1 (flash/thermometer-like, fixed reference with stepped strobe times) comparisons, each needing a latch or a transfer. Time-to-threshold encoding is inherently 1/I-nonlinear: with C≈30 fF and full-scale currents of µA, high-count codes would be separated by sub-ns differences in trip time — likely impractical at 6–10 bits with existing strobe timing generators (inference, not sourced).
- Repeated full reads with different WL voltages (read-retry style) are the only publicly available route and cost ~one tR (tens of µs on standard TLC parts) per comparison; ~255 comparisons for an 8-bit thermometer code ≈ 255 × ~40 µs ≈ 10 ms per page (illustrative arithmetic using Park's ~39 µs/sense).
- The SanDisk BNN patent is notable because it comes from a NAND vendor and frames counting simultaneously-conducting strings on one bitline as requiring the *introduction* of multi-bit sense amplifiers — evidence (by omission) that standard SAs are single-threshold.

### Gaps
- No public datasheet soft-read tR penalty numbers (µs) for a specific part were found; only the "one sensing per reference" model and MCFlash's relative latencies.
- No public evidence found that any shipping die exposes multi-strobe (multiple integration times per discharge) to the host; no patent found that applies multi-strobe time quantization to summed multi-string bitline current.
- Number of data latches per page buffer in current TLC/QLC dies (which bounds strobes storable per access) not found in a public primary source.

## Q4. What resolution do read-retry / soft-decision / Vth-distribution studies achieve on commercial parts with public commands?

### Takeaway
Using read-retry/read-offset commands, researchers resolve Vth distributions by sweeping the read reference in vendor-defined DAC steps, one full page read per step — a cell-side, binary, many-read procedure. Step sizes in mV are generally not disclosed; some vendors provide coarser/non-uniform steps and cannot go negative.

### Cited Findings
- Cai, Haratsch, Mutlu, Mai (CMU/LSI), DATE 2013: first measurement-based MLC Vth-distribution characterization using "the read retry feature present in some 2Y-nm (i.e., 20-24nm) flash chips" with Set REF / Get REF commands on an FPGA tester; bins are "one or a few tunable read reference voltage step sizes"; reads are repeated while incrementing REF; "we cannot set the read reference voltage to negative values," so the erased state cannot be characterized — [Cai et al., DATE 2013](https://past.date-conference.com/date16/files/proceedings/2013/pdffiles/09.6_1.PDF) [MEASURED-COTS]
- Later 1X-nm work swept "all possible read reference voltages" via read-retry; a footnote notes some vendors provide fewer read reference voltages near distribution peaks — [Cai et al. survey, arXiv 1808.04016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.04016) (search snippet)
- Read-retry on 160 48-layer 3D TLC chips: pages needing 16 and 21 retry steps shown; the average number of retry steps rises to 19.9 under a 1-year retention age at 2K P/E cycles, increasing tREAD by 21× on average; a fresh page needs no retry; each retry step ≈ a regular page read — [Park et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09611)
- Micron MT29F32G08CBADA exposes 8 discrete read-retry modes — [Linux MTD patch](https://lists.infradead.org/pipermail/linux-mtd/2014-January/051525.html)
- MCFlash achieved zero RBER for two-operand AND/OR/XNOR/NOT on fresh blocks of 50 COTS chips (10 per part/generation) using offsets + SBR; non-zero RBER after cycling (<0.015% after 10K P/E) — [MCFlash](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05119)

### Inferences
- These methods give a **per-cell Vth histogram via many binary reads**, not a numerical current measurement, and each level is a separate array access. They are informative for single-cell Vth resolution but say nothing about sensing a sum of string currents.

### Gaps
- Read-offset step size in mV and full range for any 3D NAND part were not found in a public source (Cai et al. use normalized DAC steps).

## Q5. arXiv 2511.12860 — what is added vs. assumed?

### Takeaway
A **simulation-only** analog 3D-NAND PIM for LLM decode that **adds 9-bit SAR ADCs, 4:1 column muxes, shift adders, a BLS (string-select) decoder, a bitline precharger and a digital reconfigurable processing unit** per plane; it sums currents of many strings on a bitline (multiple blocks driven at the same WL, bit-serial inputs on string selects), caps activation at 256 cells per bitline "due to reliability," and models no specific manufacturer.

### Cited Findings
- Title/authors/date: "Dissecting and Re-architecting 3D NAND Flash PIM Arrays for Efficient Single-Batch Token Generation in LLMs," Yongjoo Jang, Sangwoo Hwang, Hojin Lee, Sangwoo Jung, Donghun Lee, Wonbo Shim (Seoul Nat'l Univ. of Science & Technology), Jaeha Kung (Korea University); arXiv v1 17 Nov 2025 — [arXiv 2511.12860](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12860)
- Added circuitry: simulator modified to "incorporate 4:1 column multiplexers, 9-bit SAR-ADCs, and shift adders"; LV-peri = "decBLS, precharger, mux, ADC, page buffer, shiftadder"; plus WL decoder (HV-peri), and a Reconfigurable Processing Unit (ALU mode for accumulation, stream mode for normal R/W) on an H-tree; SSD controller ARM cores run softmax/LayerNorm — [arXiv 2511.12860](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12860)
- MAC topology: "a dot product is performed by flowing current through the BL"; WL decoder drives multiple blocks' target WL with V_Read, others V_Pass; inputs bit-serial on BLS lines; "the number of simultaneously activated blocks in QLC NAND flash PIM is limited to 256 cells" per bitline "due to reliability"; 128 BLSs per dot product; 4 BLSs per block, 64 blocks, 128 stacks; plane 256 rows × 2048 columns × 128 stacks — [arXiv 2511.12860](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12860)
- Precision: W8A8; 4-bit weight per QLC cell, 8-bit weight across two neighboring cells on separate bitlines (combined by shift-add); static weights in QLC region, dynamic (KV) MVMs in SLC region (SLC program 19× faster) — [arXiv 2511.12860](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12860)
- Assumptions about existing NAND: conventional read 20–50 µs; Z-NAND ~3 µs; target PIM ~2 µs/op; 2 GB/s bus; no explicit sense time, cell current or bitline capacitance values (latency given as RC/Horowitz-delay expressions; precharge energy ∝ N_col·V_pre²·(C_BL + C_string·N_row·(1−α))); page buffers mentioned only as where normal reads are latched — not stated whether reused for PIM; current variation, IR drop and summation linearity not discussed — [arXiv 2511.12860](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12860)

### Inferences
- The proposal's central operation (many independently selected strings on one bitline conducting at once, digitized to 9 bits) requires both a new array-control mode (multi-block/multi-BLS activation) and a new ADC per (muxed) bitline group — neither exists in public production-die documentation.

## Q6. Local PDF: "LLM-on-the-Palm" (ICCAD 2025) — what is added vs. existing; measured?; analog-MAC evidence?

### Takeaway
LLM-on-the-Palm is **digital near-array PIM, not analog in-NAND MAC**: it adds one small FP16×FP16 / FP32-accumulate MAC unit per plane next to the column decoder, reads weights with ordinary page reads into the existing page buffer, and adds a vendor-specific ONFI opcode plus multi-die input broadcast. All results are simulated; it cites no evidence for analog MAC because it does not use any.

## Q7. Survey of NAND-PIM / NAND-CIM architectures 2019–2026: added circuitry, sensing scheme, precision, measured vs simulated

### Takeaway
The literature splits cleanly: (1) **digital near-array PIM** (LLM-on-the-Palm, Cambricon-LLM, AiF, Lincoln, KVNAND, NVLLM) uses unmodified binary page reads and adds digital MAC logic — all simulated; (2) **analog bitline/source-line current-summing CIM** (2511.12860, NASiC, 3D-FPIM, Shim/Yu, Lue/Macronix, 3D-aCortex, SNU PWM) **always adds ADCs or integrating neuron/time-domain circuits and new array biasing** — almost all simulated, with measured silicon only on custom/test chips (UMN eNAND in 65 nm logic; Macronix 96-layer-based search chip with redesigned SSLs); (3) **binary in-flash logic** (Flash-Cosmos, ParaBit, MCFlash) is the only category measured on COTS dies, and its outputs are 1-bit.

## Q8. Physics of summation: strings sharing a bitline, limits on simultaneous activation, and measured linearity

### Takeaway
In 3D NAND a bitline connects to one string per sub-block (typically 4–8 or 5 sub-blocks per block, selected by separate SGD/SSL lines) and to thousands of strings across all blocks of a plane. Normal reads select one sub-block of one block. Simultaneous multi-block activation has been shown on COTS dies only via test mode and with substantial power/latency cost. Limits to analog summation include clamp/sense-window range, source-line bias, string-current saturation and variation, back-pattern dependence, and temperature. No public measurement of summed-string-current linearity on a commercial die was found.

### Cited Findings
- Block contains "several (e.g., 4 or 8) sub-blocks"; a bitline is shared by "thousands of NAND strings"; a plane has thousands of blocks; 48-cell strings in the tested 48L chips — [Flash-Cosmos](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.05566)
- SanDisk patent example: five sub-blocks, each with its own drain-side select lines, so "five vertical NAND strings" connect to each bitline within a block (illustrative) — [USPTO 12512156 "Artificial select gate cut for NAND"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12512156) (search summary); other patents show four strings per BL per block — [same search set, e.g. US11282581](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11282581)
- 3D NAND: within a block, strings share a GSL and source line; each string connects to one BL through an SSL; cells selected by one SSL on one WL form a page — [NASiC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23294); page size e.g. 128k BLs vs ~1k SLs (blocks) per plane — [NASiC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23294)
- PIM assumptions: 4 BLS per block (2511.12860); custom 3-SSL blocks (Shim) — [2511.12860](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12860); [Shim](https://www.memsys.io/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/p80-shim.pdf)
- **Multi-block activation cost on COTS dies**: inter-block MWS power +34% for 2 blocks, ~+80% for 4 blocks (still below erase until 4 blocks); 32-block MWS latency +36.3% over tR; intra-block 48-WL MWS +3.3% — [Flash-Cosmos](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.05566). Inter-block activation is "an uncommon practice in current NAND flash chip designs" and MWS needs a more complex WL decoder because "existing NAND chips typically support only single-wordline sensing" — [MCFlash](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05119)
- **Current-window limit**: vendor patent says parallel multi-block counting is bounded by "the amount of the resultant current that would be drawn and the level of resolution … from the available current window" — [US11328204B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11328204B2/en)
- **Source-line bias**: SanDisk's ABL sensing uses multi-pass sensing to shut off high-current cells first specifically to reduce source-line bias errors — [US7046568B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7046568B2/en)
- **Variation/back-pattern**: UMN's eNAND CIM needed "a novel back-pattern tolerant program-verify scheme" to reduce cell current variation to "less than 0.6 μA" — [UMN JSSC summary](https://www.storagenewsletter.com/?p=239086); NASiC: as current σ rises, allowed input dimension falls (128 inputs at σ = 0.15 → 8-bit ADC) — [NASiC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23294); 2511.12860 caps simultaneous activation at 256 cells/BL for reliability — [2511.12860](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12860)
- **String physics**: 3D string read current saturates with height due to poly-channel series resistance; several-kelvin self-heating gradients across the stack — [arXiv 2610.06363](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06363v1)
- **Capacitance/settling**: block source line ~16 pF → "hundreds of ns" settling for summed current (HSPICE); BL setup 12 ns only because BLs were shortened to 144 µm — [Shim et al.](https://www.memsys.io/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/p80-shim.pdf)
- **Cell current ranges in CIM studies**: Macronix 32-layer CIM prototype with 3 nm poly channel: on ≈ 2 nA, off < 1 pA (a special low-current device) — [Shim et al.](https://www.memsys.io/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/p80-shim.pdf); SNU 26 nm 2D FG cells: 8 levels 0–1.4 µA (200 nA steps), output resistance ≈ 20 MΩ in saturation, σ/µ 1.68–3.43% — [Lee & Lee 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnins.2020.571292/full)
- **Linearity evidence found**: UMN measured summation of 28 string currents to frequency on custom 65 nm silicon (snippet) — [UMN PDF](https://people.ece.umn.edu/groups/VLSIresearch/papers/2021/JSSC21_NAND.pdf); NASiC reports "distinct and linear current steps" for multibit cells in SPICE only — [NASiC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23294); SNU reports capacitor voltage linear in current difference only in a limited range (simulation) — [Lee & Lee 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnins.2020.571292/full)

### Inferences
- Distinguish: (a) **multi-WL activation within one series string** (Flash-Cosmos intra-block MWS) yields an AND — the string conducts only if every selected cell conducts; there is no current summation. (b) **Multiple independently controlled strings in parallel on one bitline** (different sub-blocks via separate SGDs, or different blocks) is the topology that sums currents; on COTS dies it has only been shown as inter-block OR via test mode, read out as one bit.
- In 2D NAND a block has one string per bitline, so in-block summation is impossible and parallel strings on a BL require multi-block selection (standard 2D architecture; not separately sourced here).
- For a 6–10-bit sum, the sense window must span ~64–1024× a unit string current while the SA is designed to discriminate a single string's ~100 nA-class current at one threshold; together with source-line bias, clamp headroom and the variation figures above, this suggests (unsourced) that even with sense-node access, linearity beyond a few bits would need calibration and redesigned bias/sensing.

### Gaps
- No public vendor data on the number of SGD/sub-blocks per block for specific current products (Samsung V9/V10, Kioxia BiCS8/10, Micron 232/276L, SK hynix 238/321L, YMTC X3/X4), beyond patents and generic statements.
- No measurement of summed multi-string current linearity, IR drop, or temperature dependence on a commercial 3D NAND die found.
- How Macronix's VLSI 2023 chip computes Hamming distance (current summation vs. digital counting) was not retrieved.
