## Key question 1 — US 2025/0342884 A1 ("Combining currents for NAND in-memory compute"): bibliographic data, topology, input application, simultaneous vs sequential summation, conversion to a number, and what is described as new

### Takeaway
US 2025/0342884 A1 (SanDisk; filed 2024-05-03; published 2025-11-06; still a pending application per Google Patents) explicitly describes the user's target operation: copies of a weight vector are programmed into several NAND strings that share one bit line (one string per sub-block), inputs are applied as analog word-line voltages, several sub-blocks' SGDs are turned on *at the same time* so that the string currents add on the bit line, and the summed current discharges a pre-charged sense-node capacitor whose voltage (or time-to-reference) is the "accumulated signal." It reuses the vocabulary and circuit model of the ordinary SanDisk sense amplifier (sense node pre-charged to ~3 V, discharged for a sensing time, compared with Vtrip), but it never states that existing commercial firmware/test modes can do this; it leaves the "comparison circuitry" that turns two accumulated signals into a number unspecified (no ADC, counter or bit-width is given), and an alternative embodiment (currents from several *different* bit lines into one sense node) implies wiring that the normal one-sense-amp-per-bit-line architecture described in the same document does not have. It contains no measured silicon data.

### Inferences
- Capability (2)+(3) (independently selected strings on one BL summed in parallel) is described via ordinary per-sub-block SGD lines, which the spec's generic 3D-NAND description already contains (FIG. 4C/4F, four SGDs per block; [0101]) — consistent with, but not a confirmation of, BiCS sub-block organization. What is *not* ordinary is turning on several SGDs of the same block at once during a read; the spec presents it as a control choice ("each sub-block gets selected"), not as a new transistor-level circuit, but nothing public shows a commercial die's state machine or test mode permits it. Stacking strings from *different blocks* ([0174]) would further require multiple blocks' word lines driven simultaneously, which conflicts with "one block per plane is selected at a time" ([0102]).
- Capability (1): FIG. 15A needs 2n distinct, simultaneously applied analog WL voltages (Vg±Vx_i) and the V_GS-ladder siblings need per-WL voltages up to ~24 V in examples — far beyond the handful of read voltages (selected-WL read level + Vread pass) a normal read applies; this implies a modified WL-bias generation scheme even though the patent does not label it "new."
- Capability (4)/(5): the accumulated signal is the analog voltage on the existing-style sense capacitor; conversion to a 6–10-bit number is left to unspecified "comparison circuitry." With only the stock 1-bit-per-strobe comparator ([0095]), a multi-bit value would need repeated strobes or repeated reads with different sense times/references — plausible from other SanDisk sensing patents (see KQ3) but not described here.
- FIG. 20B/21B/23 (currents from many bit lines into *one* sense node) contradicts the stock one-sense-amp-per-bit-line wiring ([0092]); this embodiment implies added bit-line-merging hardware.
- The ~100 nA per-string / ~10 nA difference figures and all V_GS numbers are illustrative/simulated; no silicon data is reported.

## Key question 2 — Wider SanDisk/WD portfolio on NAND MAC / CIM / multi-string current accumulation (number, title, mechanism, readout, new vs existing circuits)

### Takeaway
SanDisk/WD's NAND-CIM patents fall into four mechanism families: (A) **binary/ternary "unit-synapse" schemes (2018–2020; Choi, Chiu, Ma, Qin, Hemink, Hoang, Lueker-Boden)** that do a normal 1-bit sense per word-line pair and accumulate with a per-bit-line digital counter — sequential, and only made parallel across blocks by *adding* multi-bit sense amplifiers and decoder modifications; (B) **latch-level digital compute** (XNOR/popcount in the page-buffer latches after an ordinary read — reuses existing latches, no analog summation); (C) **analog multi-level schemes (2021–2024)** — Yanli Zhang's US 12,456,040 (inputs on bit lines, linear-region cells, currents summed on a per-sub-block *separate* source line and measured by a sense amp + ADC) and the 2024 Hofmann/Vucinic/Lueker-Boden/New family (inputs on word lines, multiple sub-blocks' strings summed on a bit line into a sense-node capacitor, differential positive/negative stacks); (D) **architecture-level** (bonded memory/CMOS die stacks doing compute in the CMOS die; a processor stacked on a CBA NAND tile). Every analog-summation family either states or implies circuitry that ordinary read paths lack (multi-bit SA, separate source lines with SL sensing + ADC, multi-BL-to-one-sense-node merging, many simultaneous analog WL levels); none reports measured BiCS silicon.

## Key question 3 — SanDisk/WD publications: (a) CIM on real BiCS silicon or test chips; (b) NAND sense-amplifier circuitry (current sensing, sense-node discharge timing, programmable sense time, precharge, multi-level sensing) and whether time-based sensing could act as a quantizer

### Takeaway
(a) No SanDisk/WD paper reporting measured compute-in-memory on BiCS (or any SanDisk NAND) silicon was found; the only SanDisk/WD NAND-CIM paper located (ISCAS 2020) gives *estimated* efficiency, and the Lueker-Boden group's measured-hardware papers are on ReRAM/MTJ crossbars. (b) SanDisk's own public documents (mostly patents, plus the 2008/2009 all-bit-line papers) describe the stock sense amplifier as a *current-sensing, time-integrating* circuit: a sense-node capacitor is pre-charged (~Vsense_init 3 V), discharged by the bit-line current for an "integration time," and compared with a trip voltage; the effective reference current is set by the discharge period, the same discharge can be strobed twice at different times, and trip levels can be shifted by the capacitor's second-plate voltage. These are the ingredients of a time-based quantizer, but each documented strobe yields 1 bit, the documents describe 2 strobes per discharge (not 64–1024), and timing values are fuse/register parameters set at characterization — no public interface to sweep them is documented.

### Inferences
- Physics implied by SanDisk's descriptions (my formulation, not a SanDisk equation): Iref ≈ C_SEN·(Vsense_init − Vtrip)/t_sense, so sweeping t_sense (or the second-plate/trip level) at a fixed WL bias maps an unknown summed current onto a threshold — a single-slope/time-to-threshold ADC built from one comparator and a latch. With the documented 2 strobes per discharge, one discharge gives ~1.6 bits (3 bins); 6–10 bits would need on the order of 64–1024 thresholds, i.e., dozens to hundreds of repeated sense operations with different timing/reference register values (or a counter clocked during discharge, which is not described in any SanDisk document found).
- The dynamic range problem compounds this: per-string currents of ~6–140 nA (12,456,040 examples) to ~100 nA (2024 family) summed over 2–128 strings span ~10 nA to >10 µA; a fixed C_SEN and Vtrip window covers only a limited ratio of t_sense values, so wide-range numerical readout would require t_sense spanning several decades or capacitor/reference changes.
- Because sense-time and reference values are stored "as register values"/"fuse values" from characterization, any time-based quantizer on a commercial die would depend on vendor test-mode/parameter access that is not publicly documented.

## Key question 7 (synthesis of objective) — Which SanDisk/WD-described functions rely on new circuits vs capabilities already present in commercial BiCS sensing, per capability (1)–(5)

### Takeaway
On public evidence, only the *ingredients* are described as part of ordinary SanDisk read circuitry (in SanDisk patents' generic 3D-NAND descriptions and the Kioxia/Sandisk BiCS10 ISSCC 2025 summary): WL bias generation for one selected WL plus Vread pass voltages, per-sub-block SGD lines, and a per-bit-line current-integrating sense node with characterization-set (fuse/register) timing and 1-bit strobes. Every SanDisk description of simultaneous multi-string current summation with numerical readout either explicitly adds circuitry (multi-bit SA, modified decoders, modified counters, separate source lines + SL ADC) or depends on undefined "comparison circuitry" and non-standard bias modes (several SGDs of a block on at once; many simultaneous analog WL levels). None is documented as user-accessible on a commercial die.

### Inferences
- The most "existing-circuit-compatible" SanDisk-described route to y = Σ w_i x_i with a multi-bit result would be: (i) binary/ternary weights and inputs (BNN family) with sequential 1-bit reads and digital counting, or (ii) the 2024 family's sub-block summation read out by repeated 1-bit strobes at stepped sense times — the latter is an inference from combining two separate SanDisk documents, not something any single SanDisk document claims works on a shipping die.
- Distinctions to preserve when reporting: multi-WL activation inside one string (FIG. 15A cells, BNN word-line pairs) ≠ multi-string parallel summation; sequential WL-by-WL (Zhang, BNN) ≠ simultaneous; binary reads + counting (BNN, latch XNOR) ≠ numerical current measurement.

### Gaps
- No public BiCS test-mode, parameter-register map, command set, or timing table was found with which to check whether simultaneous multi-SGD selection, per-WL analog biasing, analog sense-node access, or sense-time sweeping is reachable on a commercial die; no command bytes are known or given here.
