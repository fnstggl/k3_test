## 2. Time budget per dependent stage

Single-stream decode cannot overlap the stages of one token: per-token time >= depth x stage latency.

| tok/s | per token | per MVM stage (698) | per stage incl. reductions (1396) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 10.00 ms | 14.33 us | 7.16 us |
| 500 | 2.00 ms | 2.87 us | 1.43 us |
| 1000 | 1.00 ms | 1.43 us | 0.72 us |

Consequence: at 500 tok/s every matrix-vector stage - input delivery, array settle, conversion, partial-sum reduction, scaling and the hop to the next stage - must complete in about 2.9 us (1.4 us if each normalisation/top-k is its own synchronisation). Commodity NAND page reads take 20-50 us, so a commodity read cannot sit on the per-token critical path even once per layer.

## 3. Storage floor: which memory can even hold K3 at the price target

Minimum stored bits (experts MXFP4 as released, always-active weights MXINT8): 1.504 TB.

| memory class | area (mm2) | volume cost, normalized pricing | 2026 shortage pricing |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 3D NAND QLC (digital storage density, 28.5 Gb/mm2) | 422 | $52 | $376 |
| DRAM (DDR5/LPDDR5X $/GB) |  | $4,511 | $29,323 |
| HBM3E ($/GB) |  | $18,045 | $22,556 |
| SRAM (N3E HD macro 31.8 Mib/mm2) | 360,776 | $109,326 |  |

Only 3D NAND-class density (many layers of cells over one silicon footprint) holds the model for under $500. DRAM is ~40x and HBM ~150x too expensive per bit. This is the single hard reason to look at NAND at all: the weights must live in a memory whose cost is set by stacked layers, not by lithography area. Everything below is about extracting the needed work from such a memory fast enough.

## 4. Throughput floors at the target rate

If weights are moved out of the memory (digital), every active bit is sensed and transported each token. If they stay in place (analog), the bit line sums C products per conversion and only partial sums move.

| requirement | 100 tok/s | 500 tok/s | 1000 tok/s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| weight bytes moved (MXINT8 dense), B/s | 8.31 TB/s | 41.6 TB/s | 83.1 TB/s |
| NAND sense amps busy, tR = 3 us (Z-NAND class) | 199 M | 997 M | 1.99 G |
| NAND sense amps busy, tR = 0.5 us (hypothetical) | 33.2 M | 166 M | 332 M |
| analog conversions/s (E, C=128, MLC) | 575 G/s | 2.88 T/s | 5.75 T/s |
| 8-bit ADCs needed at 50% utilisation | 23 k | 115 k | 230 k |
| ADC area for those (mm2, realistic ADC) | 10 | 52 | 105 |
| attention FLOP/s at 32K | 16.4 TFLOP/s | 82.1 TFLOP/s | 164 TFLOP/s |
| KV read bandwidth at 32K (BF16) | 90.6 GB/s | 453 GB/s | 906 GB/s |
| attention FLOP/s at 128K | 65.7 TFLOP/s | 329 TFLOP/s | 657 TFLOP/s |
| KV read bandwidth at 128K (BF16) | 362 GB/s | 1.81 TB/s | 3.62 TB/s |
| attention FLOP/s at 1024K | 526 TFLOP/s | 2.63 PFLOP/s | 5.26 PFLOP/s |
| KV read bandwidth at 1024K (BF16) | 2.9 TB/s | 14.5 TB/s | 29 TB/s |

Analog in-situ compute cuts the sensing work by the number of summed contributors: 5.75 G conversions per token instead of 665 G sensed bits. That ~20-50x reduction is the physical reason analog CIM can beat digital NAND streaming at equal sensing technology; it is paid for in precision (section 6).

## 6. Requirement audit (question every requirement)

| requirement | set by | verdict | consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| all 2.78 T weights resident | model (routing picks 16 of 896 experts per layer per token, decided ~us before use) | physics of the workload | every expert must be reachable in < ~3 us; 'cold' tiers with 20-50 us reads cannot be on the path |
| BF16 always-active weights | release format | convention | MXINT8 re-quantisation measured at 0.12-0.29 % output error (budget 1.1 %): 8-bit is sufficient; 4-bit (4.8 %) is not without re-training |
| MXFP8 activations | K3 QAT (model card) | model | 8-bit inputs are what K3 was trained to tolerate; fewer input bits are unvalidated |
| exact digital arithmetic | habit | convention | K3 already absorbs MXFP8 activation noise (1.1-2.7 % per layer); analog error below ~25 % of that is in the noise |
| signed weights need two cells | differential sensing | design choice | offset/reference-column coding halves cells but couples error to the reference; kept differential here |
| fast attention over 1M tokens | product target | requirement | 2.6 PFLOP/s + 14.5 TB/s at 500 tok/s; dynamic data cannot live in NAND (endurance), so this is a DRAM/SRAM + logic problem whatever stores the weights |
| sequential depth 698 | model architecture | model | single-stream only; batching or pipelining across users does not help one stream; speculative decoding is the only depth multiplier |
| per-layer synchronisation | GPU software stack (84 us/layer measured-calibrated) | implementation | not physical: a hard-wired dataflow pays ~0.1-1 us per stage instead |
| <$500 unit cost | business target | target | storage alone forces 3D-NAND-class bits; the attention engine + KV memory for >=128K at 500 tok/s is the cost driver that NAND cannot remove |

## 7. Step-function ladder (one physical change per rung)

Analog knobs: C=128, 2-bit cells, 8-bit ADC (default (no DSE)). Silicon cost = volume silicon, normalized pricing, KV sized for 128K; packaging/board/NRE excluded (unknown).

| rung | from | change | tok/s 32K | tok/s 128K | tok/s 1M | J/tok 128K | silicon $ (128K) | limiter 128K | precision |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| R0 | - | commodity TLC NAND, weights streamed over ONFI to an external accelerator (A0) | 0.228 | 0.228 | 0.228 | 49.8 | 252 | nvm_io_bandwidth | exact_digital |
| R1 | R0 | compute next to every plane, SLC mode, commodity planes (A1) | 1.24 | 1.24 | 1.24 | 13.8 | 415 | nvm_sense_throughput | exact_digital |
| R2 | R1 | redesign the array: 256 small low-latency SLC planes per die, digital (A2) | 259 | 144 | 22.1 | 5.87 | 2,520 | critical_path | exact_digital |
| R3 | R1 | instead: sum currents of many cells on the bit line (analog), commodity planes (B-mp) | 5.35 | 5.23 | 4.36 | 2.65 | 700 | critical_path | unknown(precision study not run) |
| R4 | R3 | small analog planes (256x2048), C=128, 2-bit cells, 1-bit input steps (B) | 208 | 112 | 21.2 | 0.733 | 1,182 | critical_path | unknown(precision study not run) |
| R5 | R4 | one-pass time-encoded input, analog accumulation of all 8 input bits (E) | 358 | 145 | 22.1 | 0.623 | 1,182 | critical_path | unknown(precision study not run) |
| R6 | R5 | 4x larger die-level ADC pool (2048/die, BL-held samples) | 382 | 149 | 22.2 | 0.62 | 1,275 | critical_path | unknown(precision study not run) |
| R7a | R6 | attention engine sized for 128K with HBM (1.5 PFLOP/s, 5 HBM3E stacks) | 716 | 448 | 99.9 | 0.626 | 3,650 | critical_path | unknown(precision study not run) |
| R7b | R6 | instead: KV cache in on-die SRAM, FP8 KV + FP8 attention math (1.5 PFLOP/s) | 767 | 538 | 142 | 0.308 | 1,529 | critical_path | unknown(precision study not run) |
| R8 | R7b | optimistic array physics (0.3 us WL setup, 32 ns steps), folded MLA, folded norms | 1.63e+03 | 1e+03 | 218 | 0.215 | 1,454 | critical_path | unknown(precision study not run) |
| R9 | R8 | speculative decoding, k=1 drafts, dense weights replicated x2 (hypothetical 1-layer drafter, DSpark-measured acceptance, measured K3 routing locality) | 2.06e+03 | 1.11e+03 | 210 | 0.226 | 1,482 | critical_path+draft | unknown(precision study not run) |

Reading the ladder: the big multipliers are (i) leaving commodity page geometry (R1->R2, R3->R4: ~100x, it is the plane/word-line size that sets latency, not the cell), (ii) summing in the analog domain so sensing work drops by C (R2 vs R5 at equal context), and (iii) sizing the attention engine to the context (R7). Buying KV bandwidth as on-die SRAM instead of HBM capacity (R7b vs R7a) gives the same speed for less than half the silicon cost at 128K, but SRAM cost grows linearly with context. Faster array physics (R8) is the last ~2x. Speculative decoding helps far less on weight-stationary arrays than on GPUs because each extra verified token is another array operation on shared (dense) and hot (expert) arrays; real K3 routing reuses experts across neighbouring tokens (max 4-6 of 8 tokens on one expert in layers 1-3).
